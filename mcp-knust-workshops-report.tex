\section{Executive Summary}
% ============================================================

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{What MCP is:} a multidisciplinary postgraduate-research and outreach unit in the Department of Physics, College of Science, KNUST (Kumasi, Ghana), focused on Mathematical \& Computational Physics as a complement to experimental work, with four declared research pathways: Materials Modelling; Quantum Science \& Technology (QST); Smart Renewable Energy; and Computational Seismology/Hydro-Geological Modelling.
  \item \textbf{Flagship activity:} the \href{https://physics.knust.edu.gh/mcp/2026}{African International Conference on Quantum Computing and Simulation \textless AoC\textbar Q\textgreater\ 2026} (13--20 February 2026, KNUST), themed ``Quantum for Impact: Fostering Education, Research, and Collaboration Toward the SDGs'', positioned as the follow-up to ELAIS-QST~2024 and the IYQ~2025 closing ceremony in Ghana.
  \item \textbf{Programme family:} RealMaths (applied-math graduate training), ELAIS-QST (6-month e-learning quantum school), GQuantum Education (senior-high-school quantum outreach), PSI-SSP (Perimeter Institute satellite school), \textless QoA\textgreater\ (Quantum Touch Africa network), plus active conference, outreach, and collaboration news flows.
  \item \textbf{Survey yield:} 40+ directly relevant workshops/conferences found across the four pathways for 2025--2026, including Africa-specific quantum events (AfriQA~2026 Kigali, PWF Morocco, QML4Africa, QTML Stellenbosch, SOAPHYS, ASP2026), energy-modelling schools in Africa (EMP-A, openmod South Africa, PyPSA meets Earth), materials-modelling schools (ICTP MARVEL college, CHASAM), and seismology/ML workshops (ML4SEG, SEG IWMG, EarthScope SSBW).
  \item \textbf{Highest-value near-term targets for MCP affiliates:} AfriQA~2026 (abstract deadline 30 Sep 2026; Ghana is a named research node), EQTC~2026 Dublin (abstracts 11 Sep 2026), QTML~2026 Stellenbosch (6--11 Dec 2026), PDEs \& Randomness (fully funded, AIMS Kigali, deadline 15 Sep 2026), and ASP2026 Nairobi.
  \item \textbf{Caveat:} a cluster of low-visibility ``International Conference on Computational Physics...'' events (WASET, APSTE, Research Foundation, Research Plus, Research Leagues) show aggregator-style patterns (identical templates, generic scopes, pay-to-publish signals) and are flagged \emph{low confidence / possibly predatory} in the tables.
\end{itemize}

\section{What MCP Is (About MCP page)}

Source: \href{https://physics.knust.edu.gh/mcp/about-mcp}{physics.knust.edu.gh/mcp/about-mcp} (confidence: high, official page).

The \textbf{Mathematical \& Computational Physics (MCP)} unit sits in the \textbf{Department of Physics, College of Science, Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST)}, PMB, Kumasi, Ghana. The page frames MCP as Ghana's answer to the technology-capacity gap:

\begin{quote}
``Ghana faces significant challenges in fostering advanced technological development and driving innovative industrialization\,\ldots\ MCP\,\ldots plays a critical role in complementing experimental and field-based work, offering computational solutions and prototypes that enhance efficiency across socio-economic and industrial sectors.''
\end{quote}

\textbf{Significance claim:} Africa/Ghana remain vulnerable to technological deficits; computational physics underpins solutions aligned with the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs).

\subsection*{Four Research Pathways (verbatim)}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em]
  \item Materials Modelling
  \item Quantum Science \& Technology
  \item Smart Renewable Energy
  \item Computational Seismology or Hydro-Geological Modelling
\end{enumerate}

\subsection*{Career Options Listed}
Research \& academia (research scientist, lecturer, professor); renewable energy (analyst, energy-systems modeller); materials science (computational materials engineer); mining/geological modelling (computational geoscientist); healthcare/biomedical (medical physicist, computational biophysicist); quantum computing (quantum research scientist, quantum engineer); software development \& simulation (simulation engineer, computational programmer).

\subsection*{Differentiating Skills}
Analytical/problem-solving ability; programming (Python, MATLAB, C++); computational modelling and simulation expertise.

\section{Part I: Synthesis}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item MCP is simultaneously a \textbf{research unit} (4 pathways), a \textbf{training pipeline} (RealMaths, PSI-SSP, ELAIS-QST), an \textbf{outreach engine} (GQuantum SHS), and an \textbf{event organiser} (AoC\textbar Q, QoA network).
  \item Quantum dominates the visible output: at least 5 of 8 programme entries are quantum-focused, consistent with the IYQ~2025 momentum and Ghana's ambition to host a quantum ``Centre of Excellence''.
  \item Internationalisation is structural: ICFO (Spain), Perimeter (Canada), Fraunhofer, Jülich, Freiburg and Saarland (Germany), L'Aquila (Italy), Quantinuum (UK), UKZN (South Africa): giving MCP ready-made mobility routes for students.
  \item The unit both \emph{runs} and \emph{attends} events (Italy water workshop, mini-ASESMA, GSA-KNUST): Part~II below maps the external opportunity space for the same audiences.
\end{itemize}

\section{Coverage Limits \& Method Caveats}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{``Whole internet'' is bounded.} The survey covers the \emph{English-language, search-indexed} web surface. Francophone, Lusophone and non-indexed African events (and events announced only via mailing lists or WhatsApp) are under-represented. The PWF Morocco and SOAPHYS entries are the main non-anglophone finds.
  \item \textbf{Snapshot, not live.} All deadlines were captured on \today. They decay: verify on the linked page before applying. The companion \texttt{mcp-deadlines.ics} and the Next-90-Days dashboard are the actionable layer.
  \item \textbf{``Watch 2027'' rows are unconfirmed.} EMP-A 2027, AfriQA 2027, SOAPHYS 2027, Qiskit Global Summer School 2027 and similar recurring series had \emph{not} published their 2027 calls at capture time; they are marked ``watch'' and must be re-checked.
  \item \textbf{Two abstract authors unnamed.} The thio-functionalised MIP-202 (Hg/As) and thiol-UiO-66(TCA) (Pb/Hg) abstracts were supplied without author names; they are matched to the MOF--DFT cluster generically. Confirm authorship before assigning.
  \item \textbf{Member matching is profile-based, not eligibility-checked.} A member listed against a grant may still fail a formal criterion (age, PhD year, nationality). See the eligibility gotchas in Part~IX.
  \item \textbf{Predatory-conference flags.} The WASET / APSTE / Research Foundation / Research Plus / Research Leagues cluster shows aggregator patterns (identical templates, generic scopes, fast acceptance, pay-to-publish). Treat as \emph{low confidence}; prefer society-run venues.
  \item \textbf{Two KNUST pages are genuinely dead} (GQuantum-ToT, AOCIQ Conference Report): retained in Part~I as documented broken menu links; the Wayback Machine holds \emph{no} snapshots of either, so no archived content could be recovered.
\end{itemize}

\section{Regeneration Pipeline \& Maintenance}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Data source:} \texttt{mcp-opportunities.csv} (164 rows: category, name, description, deadline text, ISO deadline, members, URL) is the machine-readable master extracted from the report's master table.
  \item \textbf{Generated artefacts:} \texttt{mcp-deadlines.ics} (75 dated deadlines as calendar events), \texttt{dashboard.tex} (next-90-days table), \texttt{action-cards.tex} (per-member lists): all produced by \texttt{build\_assets.py} from the CSV/table.
  \item \textbf{Link health:} \texttt{check-links.sh} re-verifies every URL in the \texttt{.tex} and reports non-200s; schedule it monthly (cron) so rot is caught early.
  \item \textbf{Update loop:} edit the master table (or CSV) $\rightarrow$ re-run \texttt{build\_assets.py} $\rightarrow$ re-run \texttt{check-links.sh} $\rightarrow$ \texttt{pdflatex} $\times$3.
\end{itemize}
